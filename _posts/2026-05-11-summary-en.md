## [JWST Finds Rare Non-Rotating Massive Galaxy in Early Universe](https://www.ithome.com/0/948/527.htm) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have discovered that galaxy XMM-VID1-2075, formed less than 2 billion years after the Big Bang, shows no signs of rotation, challenging standard galaxy evolution models. The finding was published in Nature Astronomy on May 4, 2026. This discovery challenges the long-held assumption that galaxies acquire rotation early in their formation, suggesting that some massive galaxies can evolve into slow-rotating states much faster than previously thought. It forces a re-evaluation of galaxy formation theories and the role of mergers in the early universe. The galaxy, named XMM-VID1-2075, is one of the most massive known in the early universe, with several times the stellar mass of the Milky Way, and has already stopped forming new stars. The team used JWST to measure internal motions, finding random stellar motions instead of ordered rotation, similar to some nearby massive galaxies.

rss · IT之家 · May 10, 11:50

**Background**: Galaxies are expected to acquire angular momentum from infalling gas and gravitational interactions, causing them to rotate. Over billions of years, galaxy mergers can either enhance or cancel out rotation, leading to slow-rotating galaxies in the local universe. However, this process was thought to take much longer than the age of the universe at the time this galaxy was observed (less than 2 billion years).
